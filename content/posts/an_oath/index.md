“If beneath things there were to hide a bottomless void, never to be filled, what would life be other than despair? If this were the case—if no sacred bond were to unite humanity, if generations were to renew themselves one after another like the foliage of forests, if they were to be extinguished one by one like the birdsong fading in the woods; if they were to traverse the world as a ship crosses the ocean, or as the wind crosses the desert, as a blind and sterile action; if an eternal oblivion, perpetually ravenous, were to stalk its prey, and there were no force capable of tearing it away—how empty and desolate life would be"



Drawing from Kierkegaard’s meditation on faith, sacrifice, and the paradox of the ethical in Fear and Trembling, the piece stages a sonic rite of surrender and tension.
Just as Abraham stands before the unresolvable, poised between duty and the absolute unknown, the performers navigate the fragile threshold between individual agency and the weight of the collective. The act of amplification becomes an instrument of exposure, a means through which
presence is stretched, fragmented, and
ultimately entrusted to the shifting
architectures of spaces and resonance
Fear And Trembling 
Soren Kierkegaard
